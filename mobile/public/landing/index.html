Mnemonics

One story, carried through three steps.

You do not learn a mnemonic for each item from zero. The picture you learn for a radical comes back in the kanji, and the kanji story comes back in the word.

1

Learn the shapes

Each radical is one picture that looks like its shape.

儿human legs
Two strokes sweep down and out, like a pair of legs.
冖cover
A blanket thrown over a sleeping cat. Nothing pokes out.
士samurai
A man in armour. The long top line is his wide shoulders.

The shapes make the kanji

The kanji story uses only the radicals you know.

売
sell
儿 human legs冖 cover士 samurai
Meaning mnemonic

A samurai too proud to be seen throws a cover over his face and takes to the road on his own human legs, where the only work left is to sell.

Reading mnemonic

The proud samurai hides his face under the cover and trudges the road crying 「ばい」, buy, buy, since the only work left to him is to sell (売).

3

The kanji makes the word

The same samurai comes back to give you the reading.

売る
to sell
売 sell
Meaning mnemonic

The sell kanji and the to sell word are exactly the same, so they mean the same thing.

Reading mnemonic

Face hidden under his cover, the proud samurai walks the road with a stuffed kangaroo, calling ooh, Roo, 「うる」, at every gate, since all that is left is to sell (売る) it.

Grammar

Grammar gets the same treatment.

Each grammar point has a full lesson and a story mnemonic. Then it goes into the same review queue as your kanji and words.

たことがある
have done before (experience)
Verb-た + ことがある
Lesson

Your friend asks if you have ever eaten sushi. Not "do you eat sushi", but "have you ever". Japanese says it with a small noun, こと, after a verb in its past form: "the thing of having eaten exists."

Mnemonic

Han the half-and-half chef unbuttons his coat to show half a taco toga, shell crumbs on the hem: proof he has done it before. 日本に行ったことがある。
