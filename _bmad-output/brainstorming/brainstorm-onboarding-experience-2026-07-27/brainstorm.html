TECHNIQUE 01

Persona Journey

40 ideas

Ten archetypes walk into the app on day one. Each one is confused by something completely different — and each confusion is a card. Solved in-character, not in the abstract.

熱

The Anime Kid

16. Wants to drop subtitles. Zero patience for theory. Will bounce off a lecture.

  • First card isn't instructions — one anime-style Japanese line with furigana and "you'll read this in 6 weeks." Tap reveals the breakdown. Teach by promise, not explanation.
  • Open with the payoff: the actual three kanji from today's first lesson, big, before SRS is mentioned at all.
  • Two-panel manga card answering "why does the app show the same kanji twice" — one mask for meaning, one for on'yomi.
  • Swipe up = "I know this, hide this card family forever." A gesture that teaches the deck is disposable — not a Skip All button.
  • Wordless card: the radical → kanji → vocab chain animates itself into a tiny tree. Zero sentences.
  • Zero-text composition: 日 + 本 → 日本 → Japan. Compositional kanji taught in three glyphs.
  • Cards re-openable from a "How this works" shelf in Browse — dismissing costs nothing.
  • Deliberate mis-teaching card: show a wrong answer being marked wrong, so the first real one isn't a shock.
  • Pre-empt the top interleaving confusion: "The app will show you things you haven't finished learning. That's on purpose."
  • Anti-lecture hard rule: 12 words max of body text. If it needs more, it's two cards.
燐

The Duolingo Refugee

28. Trained on streaks, gems and hearts. Reads their absence as "this app has no motivation."

  • Name the competitor's model and break it: "There's no streak here. Here's what replaces it." → the goal ring.
  • Goal-ring card showing the ring overshooting past 100% with "that's allowed" — the single most confusing visual in the app.
  • The ring ratchets weekly, upward only, and is not a capacity ceiling.
  • "Missing a day costs you nothing" — explicitly de-weaponizes the guilt loop other apps install.
  • Say where the streak number went: days-studied lives in Stats, on purpose.
  • Reframe: reviews aren't punishment for forgetting. They are the product.
  • One item at all five SRS stages on one screen — Apprentice→Guru→Master becomes spatial, not vocabulary to memorize.
  • Weakness biasing in one line: "Stuff you keep missing comes back sooner. The app watches your misses, not the clock."
  • An offline card the user can test right now: "Turn on airplane mode and keep going."
  • Negative-space onboarding: a card listing what this app deliberately doesn't have — no gems, no lives, no hearts — and why.
鍛

The WaniKani Veteran

34. Knows SRS, radicals, Guru. Would be insulted by a beginner deck — but needs the diffs.

  • Import-aware deck: if a WaniKani/Anki/Bunpro import was detected, collapse to 3 cards led by "here's what's different."
  • A diff card, not a tutorial: two columns — "WaniKani does X / this app does Y" — for radical naming, kun'yomi, review ordering.
  • The choice veterans will hunt for: kun'yomi isn't drilled on the kanji card — it's learned through vocabulary.
  • The unified interleaved queue: no batching by item type. Everything mixed.
  • Fast-path toggle on card 1 — "I've done SRS before" — swaps the whole deck for the advanced one.
  • Glossary strip: Apprentice, Guru, Master, radical, on'yomi, Guru+ coverage. The app's vocabulary in one place.
  • Passages unlock at 90% Guru+ coverage — otherwise the reading content is invisible and feels missing.
  • Plain-words card for the 0.7 FSRS + 0.3 weakness blend, with a "see the math" expander for min-maxers.
遍

Heritage · Traveler · Grandparent · Commuter

Speaks but can't read · needs politeness before kanji · needs 200% text · has 12 offline minutes and one hand.

  • The deck asks exactly one branching question — "can you already speak some?" — and reshapes itself. One question, not a survey.
  • Politeness: です/ます vs plain, framed as "you'll sound rude by accident — here's the one rule that prevents it."
  • Counters: Japanese counts things differently depending on what they are. One visual, three counters, no memorization ask.
  • Three scripts as three jobs, not three alphabets: hiragana is glue, katakana is foreign, kanji is meaning.
  • Kana lookalikes: シ/ツ, ソ/ン, ね/れ/わ side by side with stroke direction animated — the highest-frequency beginner error.
  • Pitch accent as permission to ignore: "you don't need this yet, but here's why 橋 and 箸 differ."
  • Accessibility-first rule: readable at 200% text scale, tap zones as large as swipe, a Next button always present so swiping is never required.
  • One-handed commuter mode: tap anywhere advances, swipe optional, no timed animation that punishes interruption.
  • The deck remembers position — resume mid-deck days later, never restart at card 1.
  • Every card carries a persistent "Start learning" escape. The deck is opt-out at any point and never blocks.
  • Trickle, don't dump: 3 cards now, the rest fired the first time each concept appears — first wrong answer fires the SRS card, first Guru fires the stage card.
  • For the serial quitter: "Week 3 is when people quit. Here's what to do when reviews pile up" — with the load-reducing button embedded in the card.
TECHNIQUE 02

Alien Anthropologist

36 ideas

A baffled outsider observes the app, and then observes the language. It turns out both are full of things that are perfectly sensible once explained — and completely insane until then.

Field notes A — observing the application

OBS-41

There is a tab for talking to a robot that needs a key from a company, inside an app that proudly needs no account. → card honestly explaining Talk is the one online feature, and why BYOK.

OBS-42

It teaches shapes that are not words before it teaches words. Radicals need justification or beginners read them as busywork.

OBS-43

The furigana silently vanishes and nobody warned the human. → card: auto-hide keys on vocabulary mastery, overridable in Settings.

OBS-44

One kanji is graded by the worst of two answers. Feels unfair unless stated: "we grade the weaker half, because that's the half that'll fail you."

OBS-45

A keyboard key is a navigation control. → card teaching the Return-to-advance rhythm — type, Return, Return — that makes reviews fast.

OBS-46

Browse is full of things you're not allowed to learn yet. → reframe Browse as a map, not a menu.

OBS-47

No accounts means losing the phone loses everything. → an honest backup/export card, shown before the user has anything to lose.

OBS-48

Nothing tells the human how long this takes. → an honest horizon ("N5 in about X months") computed from their chosen intensity.

OBS-49

Humans pick "intensity" before knowing what a lesson feels like. → re-offer the choice as a card after the first lesson, when it's informed.

OBS-50

Free, no ads, no subscription reads as suspicious. → a "who made this and why" card, because trust is an onboarding problem.

OBS-51

Five tabs, and a beginner needs one. → a card granting explicit permission to ignore four tabs for the first week.

OBS-52

What happens if I answer everything wrong on purpose? → card: the app adapts down, it doesn't punish.

OBS-53

What does a 140% day mean on the ring? → a card that actually answers overshoot.

OBS-54

Lessons and reviews are different acts feeding one ring. → separate them visually exactly once.

Field notes B — observing the language

OBS-55

This species writes one language in three scripts simultaneously and mixes them mid-sentence. The single best framing for why Japanese looks scary.

OBS-56

On'yomi vs kun'yomi told as a history card — borrowed from Chinese vs native — rather than a rule to memorize.

OBS-57

Kanji aren't an alphabet. Closer to emoji with a fixed vocabulary — and they compose.

OBS-58

There are no spaces between words. Show one real sentence with and without artificial spaces.

OBS-59

The verb lands at the end — you can't know if it's a yes or a no until then.

OBS-60

Particles as sticky labels: tiny words that label each noun's job, shown physically attached to a sentence.

OBS-61

は is pronounced wa when it's a particle — the first thing that makes beginners think they're broken.

OBS-62

Numbers change shape depending on what you count — 本 / 枚 / 匹.

OBS-63

No plurals, no articles, subject often dropped. "Japanese leaves out what you consider mandatory."

OBS-64

Politeness is grammatical, not tonal — you conjugate respect.

OBS-65

Day-one confidence: katakana signals foreign, so you already know hundreds of words — コーヒー, テレビ.

OBS-66

Instant win: you already know some kanji — 一二三, 山, 川 — shown as pictures.

OBS-67

Stroke order matters for lookup and legibility, but you won't be graded on it here. Expectation set early.

OBS-68

Pitch accent exists; this app doesn't drill it; here's why that's an acceptable beginner trade.

OBS-69

Good news to counterbalance the scary: Japanese has almost no irregular verbs.

OBS-70

Honest scope: reading and speaking are different skills and this app leans toward reading — so nobody feels cheated in month 3.

OBS-71

The same kanji in a person's name is pronounced differently and even Japanese people ask. Normalize the confusion.

OBS-72

Mountain reframe: you're not learning 2,000 characters — you're learning ~200 parts that combine.

OBS-73

Delight: loanwords are mangled beyond recognition (マクドナルド) — and it teaches katakana vowel lengthening.

OBS-74

Onomatopoeia is a real grammatical category here. A delight card, explicitly optional.

OBS-75

Every card carries a real curriculum item pulled live from curriculum.db — the "how it works" deck doubles as first exposure.

TECHNIQUE 03

One Feature Only

27 ideas

You may ship exactly one card. Everything else is deleted. Pick it — then make it unbelievably good. The constraint turned out to be the most productive part of the session: it didn't just choose a card, it reorganised the whole idea.

The card that survives
日 + 月 → 明 → 明日

One animated composition. It teaches the whole app model, the dependency graph, and one real word — with no sentences at all.

Rival 77The first-wrong-answer card. Everything else is discoverable; being marked wrong is where people quit.
Rival 78A live mini-review — you actually answer one real item inside the card. Onboarding by doing, one item deep.
Rival 79A promise with a date: "On [date] you'll read this sentence," derived from their intensity pick. Screenshot-able.
  • Build it from the real review UI components, not a mockup — transfer to the real screen is 1:1 and it can't drift.
  • The example item is the user's actual next lesson item, read live from curriculum.db.
  • Shareable as an image — the only share surface in the app that isn't a streak brag.
  • Replayable forever from Home overflow, showing a quiet "seen 3×" so it never feels like a wall.
  • Hand-verified at 200% font scale and in dark mode — polish one card can afford where twelve cannot.
  • Exactly one interaction: a single drag that builds the kanji out of its radicals.

What the constraint deleted — and what it saved

Keep-test: does not knowing this cause a support question or a quit?

pitch accent · stroke order · onomatopoeia · counters → all move to Browse item pages.  Survivors: three scripts · on'yomi vs kun'yomi · no spaces between words.

  • If only one card survives, every other concept must become just-in-time — no concept gets a card until the user actually hits it.
  • Build a small "explain this" primitive: an info affordance on the goal ring, the SRS stage chip, the furigana toggle, and the two kanji card types. Each opens one card. The deck becomes a library, not a sequence.
  • The deck is really an index: Settings gets "How this works," listing every card. Onboarding shows three.
  • Kill the deck entirely: a 45-second first lesson that is the tutorial — first items 日, 一, 人, with the surrounding copy teaching the app.
  • Storage: a card_seen table in user.db keyed by card id — cards can be re-shown, skipped, or reordered later without migration pain.
  • Dismissal is meaningful and final: swiping a card away marks it seen and the app never re-fires it unprompted. No nagging, one visible path.
  • "Skip all, I know how apps work" on card 1 actually skips and marks every intro card seen — while leaving the Settings library intact.
  • Length cap: the intro deck must be shorter than the app's own load-to-ready time. If it's longer, it's too long.
  • The last card isn't a summary — it's the Start button in disguise, so finishing the deck and starting to learn are the same tap.
  • No analytics exist (offline, no accounts), so the only feedback loop is asking playtesters which card they remember a week later. Design every card to be memorable in one line.
SYNTHESIS

What the 103 ideas actually said

Read together, the three techniques kept colliding on the same few points. The biggest one isn't about Japanese at all.

01The subject isn't "teaching Japanese." It's explaining the app's deliberate differences.

Almost every confusion the Alien found is a choice the app made on purpose and never announced: two-card kanji, worst-of-both grading, furigana auto-hide, ring overshoot, Browse locks, no streak, no account. The deck is a changelog against the user's expectations.

02It's not one deck. It's about five micro-decks, fired by trigger.

Ordering by when confusion strikes rather than by topic breaks the deck apart on its own — and only the first piece belongs in onboarding.

03Three ideas are one idea wearing three hats.

The card_seen table, the "explain this" info affordance, and the Settings "How this works" library are one card registry. The intro deck is just a filtered view of it.

04Teaching material and app content should be the same object.

Cards render real curriculum items using the real review components; the 45-second first lesson is the tutorial. Nothing is a mockup, so transfer is 1:1 and the deck can never drift from the app.

05Silent adaptive behaviour is the enemy.

Furigana vanishing, weakness biasing, the adaptive ring, adapting down after wrong answers — each is invisible help that reads as a bug. Every silent behaviour needs a card or a visible control.

06The personalization is already free.

onboarding_path (beginner / manual / import) and starting_jlpt_level are already written during welcome. The deck can branch on them today: beginners get the language deck, importers get the WaniKani diff deck, manual gets the glossary.

07Trust is an onboarding problem, not a marketing one.

"Free, no ads, no account" reads as suspicious — and no account means losing the phone loses everything. A who-made-this card and a backup card belong in the deck before the user has anything to lose.
