You have been walking around town all afternoon in the summer heat. You walk into a convenience store, head straight for the cold drinks, and think: "I want water."

```jp
水がほしいです。
```
*I want water.*

When you want a physical object—something you can hold, buy, or point to—you use the word ほしい.

## The mechanics of wanting

The structure for expressing what you want is simple: **Noun + が + ほしい**.

Notice the particle が. In English, we say "I want a car", treating the car as the direct object of an action. But in Japanese, ほしい is actually an *i-adjective* (a descriptive word like 大きい or 新しい). You are literally saying "to me, a car is desirable". That is why we usually use が to mark the thing you want, instead of the object particle を.

```jp
新しい車がほしいです。
```
*I want a new car.*

Because ほしい functions exactly like an adjective, it follows all the normal conjugation rules you already know. To make it negative, drop the い and add くない. To make it past tense, drop the い and add かった.

```jp
古い服はほしくないです。
```
*I don't want old clothes.*

```jp
去年、カメラがほしかったです。
```
*Last year, I wanted a camera.*

## Reading minds

There is one major catch with ほしい. Because Japanese is culturally extremely careful about not claiming to know what other people are feeling inside, you can only use ほしい for **your own desires**, or when asking someone directly about theirs.

You can declare "I want a book" or ask your friend "Do you want a book?". But you cannot state as a plain fact that your friend wants a book.

> Saying `彼女は本がほしいです` sounds presumptuous, as if you are reading her mind. Later on, you will learn specific grammar to talk about what other people seem to want, but for now, keep ほしい limited to the first and second person.

## Casual speech

When you are talking with friends or family, you drop the です completely. In real casual speech, people also frequently drop the particle が, leaving just the noun and the adjective.

```jp
お茶がほしいですか。
```
*Do you want some tea? (Polite)*

```jp
お茶、ほしい？
```
*Want some tea? (Casual)*

## Things vs. Actions

This one trips everyone up the first week, so don't worry if it takes a moment to click. You already learned how to say you want to *do* something using たい. So when do you use ほしい, and when do you use たい?

It strictly comes down to nouns versus verbs.

If you want a **thing** (a noun), use ほしい. If you want to **do an action** (a verb), use たい. Look at how these two sentences feel different:

```jp
パソコンがほしいです。
```
*I want a computer.*

```jp
パソコンを買いたいです。
```
*I want to buy a computer.*

You can never attach ほしい directly to a verb, and you can never attach たい directly to a noun. They each have their own strict territory.

Once you master asking for what you want, you will eventually want to rank those desires—hold onto this thought for when we meet 一番 (the most) to say what you want more than anything else in the world.
