You check the weather app, and the little train icon has gone red. A storm rolled through overnight, and the line into the city is dead. Someone asks why you are late, and you need to name the thing that caused the shutdown — not a person who decided it, but an event that forced it.

```jp
台風によって、電車が止まった。
```

*The trains stopped because of the typhoon.*

That is によって. You take a noun — here 台風 — and attach によって straight onto it to say 'because of this', 'by means of this', or 'through this'. It points at the thing sitting behind an outcome.

## How it works

The structure is as plain as it looks: a noun, then によって. Nothing sits between them — no の, no な, no です. If you can name it as a noun, you can feed it in.

によって does four related jobs, and they all grow from a single idea: the noun is the source or the pivot of whatever follows.

**Cause.** An event or a force produces a result. This is the 'because of' reading, and you meet it constantly in news, announcements, and any moment you want to sound a little formal about a cause.

```jp
地震によって、たくさんの家が壊れた。
```

*The earthquake destroyed many houses.*

In stiffer writing — signs, reports, official notices — によって tightens to により. It is the same word clipped short, not a new one: same meaning, drier tone, nothing else changes.

```jp
台風によって → 台風により
```

*'Because of the typhoon', then the same phrase in its formal form.*

**Means or method.** The noun is the tool or the process that gets something done. Here によって sits close to で ('with', 'using'), but it frames the noun as a whole procedure — a negotiation, a vote, a piece of research — rather than a single object in someone's hand.

```jp
会議によって、新しい予定が決まった。
```

*The new schedule was settled through the meeting.*

**The doer in a passive sentence.** When a sentence is passive and you want to name who actually performed the action, によって marks that person. The reason is bookish: older Japanese built its passives this way, and formal modern writing kept the habit. You see it most with verbs of making and writing.

```jp
彼女は祖母によって育てられた。
```

*She was raised by her grandmother.*

```jp
この歌は有名な人によって作られた。
```

*This song was made by a famous person.*

**'Depending on'.** The noun is a variable, and the rest of the sentence shifts as that variable shifts. This is the reading people miss, because English often leaves the second half implied. Japanese does not — the sentence has to end on something that moves, a word like 変わる.

```jp
人によって意見が変わる。
```

*Opinions vary from one person to the next.*

## Rules and exceptions

The noun attaches bare. Learners fresh from drilling noun-modifying の sometimes write `地震のによって` — cut that の. It is always 地震によって.

For the passive 'by', ordinary speech usually just uses に. If your teacher praises you, in conversation that is 先生に褒められた, not 先生によって褒められた. Save によって for formal writing, or for verbs of creation like 作る and 書く, where a plain に can read as a location rather than a person.

> により is not a casual option. It belongs to written and spoken formality: reports, ceremonies, the station announcement itself. Drop it into a chat with a friend and you sound like you are reading aloud from a manual.

```jp
地震により、たくさんの人が困った。
```

*The earthquake left many people in difficulty.*

One more trap: によって in the 'depending on' sense needs a real outcome clause. 'Depending on the person' is not a finished thought in Japanese any more than in English — you have to say what actually changes.

## Contrast

The closest thing you already know in this register is のみ, the formal 'only'. のみ and により surface in the same places — notices, forms, official writing — and both sound wrong across a dinner table. Their jobs differ: のみ narrows the field to a single option, while により points at a cause or a means.

```jp
この問題は会議により無くなった。
```

*This problem went away through a meeting.*

```jp
この店は現金のみ使える。
```

*This shop takes cash only.*

Swap the two and the sentences break: `会議のみ無くなった` would say 'only the meeting disappeared', and `現金により使える` would say 'usable by means of cash', which is not what a sign wants to tell you. A quick check: if the English slot takes 'only', you want のみ; if it takes 'because of' or 'by', you want により.

## One line to keep

によって hooks onto a noun to name the cause, the method, or the doer behind an outcome — により when the register is formal. Later you meet 上で, another buttoned-up connector, where the noun is a step you finish before moving on rather than the force driving the result.
