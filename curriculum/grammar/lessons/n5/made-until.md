You're waiting for a friend at a coffee shop. They text you that they're running late, and you want to reply, "I'll wait until noon." You know the words for "noon" and "wait," but you need a way to draw a line in the time.

Enter まで. It acts like a boundary marker, setting the exact point where an action stops.

```jp
昼まで待ちます。
```
*I will wait until noon.*

## Drawing the line

まで attaches directly to the end of a noun. You can think of it as meaning *until* when you're talking about time, or *as far as* when you're talking about physical space.

If you're explaining how far you plan to walk with someone, you just attach it to the location:

```jp
駅まで歩きます。
```
*I will walk as far as the station.*

Notice how the rule is exactly the same for both time and place? Japanese loves this kind of consistency. You take the limit (noon, the station), tag it with まで, and follow it up with your verb.

## The dynamic duo: から and まで

You already know that から marks a starting point (*from*). Because から sets the start and まで sets the end, they show up together constantly to express "from X to Y."

```jp
朝から晩まで遊びます。
```
*I will play from morning until evening.*

This combo works perfectly for physical distances, too. You just bracket your start and end points.

```jp
家から学校まで歩きます。
```
*I will walk from the house to school.*

> When using まで with time, the action needs to be something continuous that fills the time up to the limit, like sleeping, playing, or waiting.

## Setting limits: まで against へ

You learned earlier that へ indicates the direction you are heading. So, how is that different from using まで with a place? Let's look at them side by side.

```jp
海へ行きます。
```
*I will go toward the sea.*

```jp
海まで行きます。
```
*I will go as far as the sea.*

When you use へ, you're just pointing your compass. The sea is your destination, but it's mostly a statement of where you are headed.

When you use まで, you are emphasizing the *extent* of the journey. You're saying, "My trip continues up to the sea, and that is where the travel stops." It highlights the physical distance it takes to get there.

This particle is all about setting clear boundaries. Hold onto this concept of marking a limit—it becomes incredibly handy when we want to ask questions about those limits using `か` later on!
