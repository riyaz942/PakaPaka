You've got a big trip tomorrow and need to tell your friend you are waking up early. You know the word for "early" (早い) and the verb for "wake up" (起きる). But you can't just mash them together as `早い起きます`. That sounds like "early wakes up."

When you want an adjective to describe *how* you do an action—waking up *early*, writing *big*, running *fast*—you need to turn it into an adverb.

## How it works

For い-adjectives, making an adverb is incredibly simple: drop the final `い` and replace it with `く`.

```jp
早い → 早く
```
*early → early (as an adverb)*

Now you can attach it directly to your verb to describe how the action happens:

```jp
明日は早く起きます。
```
*I will wake up early tomorrow.*

You can use this with almost any い-adjective and verb combination. 

```jp
これを安く買いました。
彼は速く走ります。
ノートに大きく書きました。
```
*I bought this cheaply.*
*He runs fast.*
*I wrote big in the notebook.*

## The one big exception

Just like with every other rule you've learned so far, the adjective いい (good) is the rebel. Before you can change it, it reverts to its older, formal shape 良い (よい). Drop the `い` and add `く`, and you get よく.

```jp
よく勉強します。
```
*I study often.*

> `よく` is incredibly common, but notice how the translation usually becomes "often" rather than "well" in many situations. If you say よく遊びます, it usually means you play a lot, not necessarily that you are highly skilled at playing.

## A mind-blowing connection

Does dropping `い` and adding `く` feel weirdly familiar? It should.

Think about how you make an adjective negative. To say a bag isn't heavy, you change 重い to `重くない`. What you are actually doing is turning "heavy" into the adverb "heavily" (重く) and attaching it to the negative word ない (not exist).

You've been using this exact grammar rule since your very first week of adjectives without even knowing it!

## Describing things vs. describing actions

When you learn a new pattern, it's easy to accidentally overuse it. If you catch yourself making mistakes, look at what word you are actually trying to describe.

If you are modifying a noun (a person, place, or thing), keep the regular `い`.
If you are modifying a verb (an action), use `く`.

```jp
これは速いバスです。
バスは速く走ります。
```
*This is a fast bus.*
*The bus runs fast.*

In the first sentence, "fast" describes the bus itself. In the second, "fast" describes the action of running.

Keep this logic in your back pocket. We just learned how to do this with い-adjectives, but soon you'll need to know how to make na-adjectives (like 静か) do the exact same thing using に.
