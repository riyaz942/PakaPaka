You're at a welcome party for new students and someone asks where you traveled from, or a friend wants to know when your new schedule starts. How do you mark the starting point of your journey or your time?

## How it works

To express "from" a place or a time, Japanese uses the particle から (a little word that attaches to nouns to show their grammatical role). It works like a thumbtack on a map or a calendar, pinning down the exact starting point of whatever action you are talking about.

You just attach it directly to the end of the noun. Remember that Japanese particles always come *after* the word they modify. So "from here" becomes "here from" (ここから).

```jp
どこから来ましたか。
```
*Where did you come from?*

```jp
家から歩きます。
```
*I will walk from home.*

It works exactly the same way for time. If an action starts at a specific moment, just attach から to that block of time.

```jp
今日から学校に行きます。
```
*I will go to school from today.*

```jp
何時からコーヒーを飲みますか。
```
*From what time will you drink coffee?*

## Rules and exceptions

Word order with particles trips everyone up the first week — that is completely normal. Unlike English, you never put the "from" before the word. A guess like `から家` means nothing in Japanese because the particle must always be attached to the back of the noun.

> Because から marks a starting line, it feels very natural with verbs involving movement (like 行く or 来る) or continuous actions that have a clear beginning.

## Contrast

You already know a few ways to mark locations and times, and it is easy to get them mixed up. Let's look at に and から. 

Think of に as an arrow pointing at your destination. から is the starting block you push off from. Swap them, and the entire direction of your sentence reverses.

```jp
日本に行きます。
```
*I will go to Japan.*

```jp
日本から来ました。
```
*I came from Japan.*

The same logic applies to time. The particle に pinpoints an exact moment something happens, while から just tells you when the block of time begins.

```jp
朝に本を読みます。
```
*I will read a book in the morning.*

```jp
朝から本を読みます。
```
*I will read a book from the morning.*

Now you can pin down exactly where and when things start. Hold onto this starting point — soon we will meet the particle まで (until), which lets you cap the other end of the timeline!
