You're making weekend plans and you want to tell a friend what you're up to. You know how to say "I'm going to the cinema" (映画館に行く) and you know how to say "I will watch a movie" (映画を見る). But how do you combine them into a single, natural thought?

You need a way to say you are going *in order to* do something.

## How it works

You already know the particle に can mark a destination or a time. Here, it steps up to mark the **purpose** of your movement.

To build this pattern, take the verb for the action you want to do and chop off the ます. We call this the "verb stem." Then, attach に, and finish with a movement verb like 行く (to go), 来る (to come), or 帰る (to return).

- 見る (to watch) becomes 見. Add に行く and you get 見に行く (go to watch).
- 遊ぶ (to play/hang out) becomes `遊び`. Add に来る and you get 遊びに来る (come to hang out).
- 食べる (to eat) becomes 食べ. Add に帰る and you get 食べに帰る (return to eat).

Usually, you also want to say *where* you are going. You put the place at the beginning of the sentence.

```jp
映画館へ映画を見に行く。
```
*I'm going to the cinema to watch a movie.*

```jp
友達がうちへ遊びに来る。
```
*A friend is coming to my house to hang out.*

```jp
毎日、学校へ勉強しに行く。
```
*Every day, I go to school to study.*

> You might notice we used the direction particle へ for the destination (映画館へ) instead of に. While you *can* say 映画館に映画を見に行く, using に twice in the same sentence feels repetitive. Many Japanese speakers prefer へ for the destination just to keep things sounding clean.

## Rules and exceptions

This pattern is incredibly strict about how it ends. The final verb **must** be a verb of physical movement. You can't use this grammar to say "I stood up to see" or "I sat down to eat." It exists exclusively for going, coming, or returning somewhere.

```jp
妹は毎晩、晩ご飯を食べに帰る。
```
*Every night, my younger sister returns home to eat dinner.*

## Dropping it in casual speech

In fast, casual conversation, Japanese speakers often drop the purpose に entirely to save breath, smashing the two verbs straight together. You should stick to the full に行く form for polite speech or any written tests, and only recognize the dropped version when you hear it.

## Stacking with what you know

Because 行く is just a normal verb, you can plug it right into other grammar rules you've already learned, like combining it with a list of activities using たり〜たりする.

```jp
週末、友達と遊びに行く。
```
*This weekend, I'll go hang out with a friend.*

```jp
レストランへおいしいご飯を食べに行く。
```
*I go to a restaurant to eat delicious food.*

```jp
週末は、本を買ったり、映画を見に行ったりする。
```
*On the weekend, I do things like buy books and go watch movies.*

Now you can declare your own plans. But what if you want to invite someone else? "How about going to eat?" You'll need どうですか for that, which is coming up next.
