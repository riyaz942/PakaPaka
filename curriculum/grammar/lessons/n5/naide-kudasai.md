You're at home with a terrible cold, wrapped in a blanket. Your well-meaning friend walks in and goes straight for the window to let in some freezing winter air. You need to stop them politely but quickly.

You already know how to ask someone to do something using てください. Here is how you ask them *not* to.

```jp
窓を開けないでください。
```
*Please don't open the window.*

## How it works

To make a negative request, you take the negative form of the verb (the *nai* form) and attach でください.

For a regular *ru*-verb, just drop the final る and add ない:

```jp
傘を忘れないでください。
```
*Please don't forget your umbrella.*

For a *u*-verb, change the final *u* vowel sound to an *a* sound, then add ない. So 飲む (to drink) becomes 飲まない:

```jp
その水を飲まないでください。
```
*Please don't drink that water.*

And for irregular verbs, you just have to remember their shapes. 心配する (to worry) becomes 心配しない:

```jp
心配しないでください。
```
*Please don't worry.*

## The missing joint

> A very common trap is forgetting the little で in the middle. It is never `飲まないください`. The で acts like a structural joint, basically meaning "in a state of not doing [verb], please exist."

Grammatically, this is still a polite request because of ください. But because you are directly telling someone what to do (or not do), it can feel a little heavy-handed if used with a boss or teacher. With friends, coworkers, or strangers on the street, it is perfectly fine.

## Casual speech

When you are talking to family or close friends, the full polite phrase often feels too stiff. In casual Japanese, you simply chop off the ください and end the sentence on で.

```jp
今日は学校に行かないで。
```
*Don't go to school today.*

## Personal requests vs. Hard rules

You recently learned てはいけない (must not). They might seem like they do the same job, but they live in entirely different spaces.

てはいけない expresses a strict rule or prohibition. You use it to state a fact about what is forbidden.

```jp
ここに入ってはいけない。
```
*You must not enter here. (It is against the rules)*

ないでください is a personal favor. You are making a request of the person in front of you.

```jp
私の部屋に入らないでください。
```
*Please don't enter my room. (I am asking you not to)*

Hold onto this negative *nai* form shape. Soon, we'll see how swapping out the でください for something else gives you the power to say what you *must* do.
