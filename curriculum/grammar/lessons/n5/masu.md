You're sitting with a new acquaintance at a café. You want to tell them you drink coffee. You know the word for coffee (コーヒー) and the word for drink (飲む). But just saying `飲む` sounds a bit blunt, like you're talking to a close buddy. How do you dress it up for polite company?

## Making verbs polite

In English, we change our vocabulary to sound more polite. In Japanese, politeness happens at the very end of the sentence, attached directly to the verb. `ます` is your standard polite verb ending. It makes you sound respectful, safe, and friendly.

To use it, we have to prepare the verb by finding its "stem." Think of the stem as the verb's connector piece.

For many verbs, like `食べる` (to eat) or `見る` (to see), you simply drop the final `る` and attach `ます`.

```jp
私はパンを食べます。
```
*I eat bread.*

For other verbs, like `飲む` (to drink), `行く` (to go), or `買う` (to buy), the final `u` sound slides up to an `i` sound before adding `ます`. So `` `飲む` `` becomes `` `飲みます` ``, `` `行く` `` becomes `` `行きます` ``, and `` `買う` `` becomes `` `買います` ``.

```jp
コーヒーを飲みます。
```
*I drink coffee.*

```jp
先生は学校に行きます。
```
*The teacher goes to school.*

```jp
明日、水を買います。
```
*Tomorrow, I will buy water.*

And then there's `来る` (to come), which just breaks the rules and becomes `来ます`.

## Time and truth: ました and ません

Because `ます` is fused to the verb, it actually takes over the job of showing the tense (past or present) and whether the sentence is positive or negative.

You can swap `ます` for its past tense, `ました`, to talk about things that already happened.

```jp
昨日、映画を見ました。
```
*Yesterday, I watched a movie.*

To make a verb negative, swap `ます` for `ません`.

```jp
友達は来ません。
```
*My friend is not coming.*

And for the past negative (did not do), we stack them into `ませんでした`.

```jp
今日は本を読みませんでした。
```
*I did not read a book today.*

## Rules and exceptions

The `ます` ending is strictly for action words. You cannot attach it to nouns or adjectives to make them polite.

Present tense in Japanese also covers the future. `食べます` means both "I eat" (as a habit) and "I will eat" (as a future plan). You don't need any extra words for "will".

> **Modify the ending first:** Never attach `ます` directly to the dictionary form of a verb. Forms like `` `食べるます` `` or `` `飲むます` `` are incorrect. You always have to change that final sound first!

## Casual speech

When you're talking to close friends or family, you don't use `ます` at all. You just use the raw, dictionary form of the verb. So `飲みます` (polite) drops back to just `飲む` (casual).

Since this is your first real verb ending, you'll use `ます` as your default whenever you're speaking to someone you aren't close friends with. You'll get plenty of dedicated practice with the negative and past forms (`ません` and `ました`) in the upcoming lessons. For now, hold onto this concept of a verb stem — it becomes very important when we start connecting verbs together with the te-form (て形) later on.
