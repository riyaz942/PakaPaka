You're trying to set up a time to meet a friend, or you want to hear all about their new job. You need a simple way to suggest an option ("how about...") or ask for an impression ("how is...").

The word you are looking for is どう, which means *how*.

## How it works

To use this pattern, just take a noun, mark it with the topic particle は, and add どうですか. It works perfectly for offering suggestions, like a time or a place to meet.

```jp
明日はどうですか。
```
*How about tomorrow?*

It also works just as well for asking someone's opinion or impression of something.

```jp
新しい仕事はどうですか。
```
*How is the new job?*

If you are asking about something that already happened, just change the copula です (the word that works like *is/am/are*) to its past tense form, でした.

```jp
昨日の映画はどうでしたか。
```
*How was yesterday's movie?*

## Rules and exceptions

This pattern is strictly for nouns. You can use it with times, places, and things, but you cannot attach it directly to a verb to say something like "how about we go."

If you want to be more polite—for example, if you are offering a drink to a visitor at your company—there is a formal version of どう. You swap it out for いかが.

```jp
お茶はいかがですか。
```
*How about some tea?*

> Because いかが is already very polite, you will almost never use it in casual speech or with close friends. Reserve it for guests, teachers, or formal situations.

## Casual speech

When you are talking to friends, you can drop the ですか entirely. Just say どう and raise your intonation at the end to make it a question.

```jp
土曜日の夜はどう？
```
*How about Saturday night?*

## Offering things: どう vs がほしい

You already know that you can ask what someone wants using がほしい. But when you are hosting someone, you have to be careful which one you use.

```jp
お茶がほしいですか。
お茶はどうですか。
```
*Do you want some tea?*
*How about some tea?*

Asking お茶がほしいですか can sound a bit too direct, almost like a medical questionnaire or an interrogation. Offering it as a suggestion with どうですか (or いかがですか) is much softer and gives them an easy, polite way to accept or decline.

Next time someone asks for your opinion with どうですか, you'll need a way to answer them. Hold onto that thought—it becomes important when we look at と思う (I think that) later on.
