You're catching up with a friend who just got back from a trip, and you've got a dozen questions lined up — the food, the flight, whether it rained the whole time. In English you'd just say "tell me about the trip." Japanese has one small, extremely useful piece of grammar that does that exact job: について, glued directly onto whatever noun you want to ask, talk, or think about.

```jp
旅行について話しましょう。
```

*Let's talk about the trip.*

That's the whole shape of it: **noun + について** = "about / regarding [noun]." No verb changes, no conjugation to learn — you just attach it and go. English "about" is a slippery little word that can mark a topic, an approximate amount, or a direction ("walking about the park"); について only ever does the first job, which actually makes it easier to use once you stop reaching for a one-to-one English match.

## How it works

について started life as に + ついて, from a verb that means something like "to touch on" or "to be attached to" — you don't need to memorize that, but it's why the pattern feels like it's "landing on" a topic rather than describing it from a distance. All you need in practice is the shape: it bolts straight onto a noun, with nothing in between — no の, no な, nothing.

```jp
旅行 → 旅行について
```

*trip → about the trip*

Once it's attached, the whole について phrase works like an adverbial add-on: it tells a verb like 話す (to speak), 考える (to think), 知る (to know), or 質問する (to ask a question) what it's aimed at.

```jp
彼はその映画について何も知らない。
```

*He doesn't know anything about that movie.*

```jp
先生に日本の歴史について質問した。
```

*I asked the teacher a question about Japanese history.*

Notice that に is still doing its normal job in that second sentence, marking who got asked — について doesn't replace any particle you already know. It just adds "on the subject of X" on top of whatever the sentence is already doing. You'll also want について for asking about a topic in the first place, with a question word standing in for the noun.

```jp
何について話していますか。
```

*What are you talking about?*

That's the natural question that a について answer replies to — someone asks 何について, and you answer with 旅行について, 映画について, whatever fits.

There's also a more formal cousin: につき. It means exactly the same thing, "regarding," but it belongs to written notices, memos, and business Japanese rather than conversation.

```jp
宿題の問題につき、説明します。
```

*Regarding the homework problem, I'll explain it.*

You'll meet につき on posted signs and formal documents far more often than in speech — keep について for talking to people, and recognize につき when you're reading something official.

One more shape worth knowing: について can also sit in front of の and modify a noun, turning "about X" into "the [thing] about X." Compare a plain topic sentence with a noun phrase built the same way.

```jp
旅行についての話は面白かった。
```

*The story about the trip was interesting.*

Here について isn't attached to a verb at all — it's attached to 話 (story) through の, describing what kind of story it was. The underlying idea never changes; only the job the whole phrase is doing in the sentence does.

## Rules and exceptions

について is not a topic marker the way は is. は tells the whole sentence "here's what we're discussing from now on"; について tells one specific verb "here's what you're aimed at." Because they're doing different jobs, you can actually stack them — について + は gives you an "as for the topic of X..." feel, useful when you're pivoting to a new subject partway through a conversation.

```jp
その問題については明日考えます。
```

*As for that problem, I'll think about it tomorrow.*

> について needs a plain noun sitting in front of it — never a verb directly. Something like `会うについて` (trying to say "about meeting") doesn't work; you'd first need to turn the verb into a noun-shaped phrase, which is further down the road. For now, keep について glued to things, not actions.

The other trap is picking a verb that can't take a "subject matter" in the first place. について pairs naturally with verbs of talking, thinking, knowing, asking, and writing — the kind of verb that can be "about" something. It doesn't pair with verbs of motion or plain existence; "about the store" doesn't combine with 行く or ある the way it combines with 話す or 考える. If the verb can't logically have a topic, について has nothing to attach its meaning to, and native speakers will just find the sentence confusing rather than politely correcting the grammar.

It's also worth being honest that について can feel a little formal or writerly even in its plain form — in very casual chatting, people sometimes just drop the topic in with は or leave it implied by context rather than reaching for について at all. That's not a rule you need to apply yourself yet; just don't be surprised if a native speaker's casual speech has fewer について phrases than a textbook dialogue does.

Learners often reach for について when they actually mean "because," and the two have nothing to do with each other. から marks a reason — the thing that caused something — while について marks a topic — the thing being discussed. "I'm worried about the exam" sounds like it should use について, but if what you mean is "because of the exam" (the exam is the cause of the worry), that's から territory, not について. Ask yourself whether the noun is the subject matter of a conversation or the reason something happened; that question sorts the two every time.

## Contrast: について vs を

You've known を since your first lessons, and with a verb like 話す it sits in exactly the slot について wants — right after the noun, nothing in between. But を makes the noun the thing the verb acts on; について makes it the topic the verb is about. Swap one for the other and the sentence's meaning changes, not just its tone.

```jp
彼女は日本語を話した。
```

*She spoke Japanese.*

```jp
彼女は日本語について話した。
```

*She talked about Japanese.*

Same words, same slot in the sentence, and the only difference — を versus について — decides whether Japanese was the language coming out of her mouth, or the subject she was discussing. This pair trips people up for a while because both patterns look so similar on the page; the only fix is slowing down and asking "is this noun the thing itself, or the topic?"

Worth remembering too: と、から and 場合 all attach to very different structures — plain-form clauses, not bare nouns — so について doesn't compete with them the way it competes with を. を is the one particle close enough in shape to actually cause mix-ups, which is exactly why it's worth this much attention. If you can ask yourself "is this noun the thing itself, or is it what's being discussed?" before you pick a particle, the two stop colliding.

Whenever you catch yourself reaching for "about" in Japanese, check first whether you're pointing at a topic mid-sentence (について) or naming the thing itself (を) — they'll keep bumping into each other until that split becomes automatic. Once that split feels settled, について itself will start showing up everywhere: in questions, in answers, in the small talk that opens almost any conversation, because "what's this about" is one of the most useful questions there is.

Down the line you'll meet によると, which sounds related but marks something else entirely: not what you're discussing, but whose word you're taking it on.
